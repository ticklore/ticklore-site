Our story

We think a ticket should outlast the night it was made for.

Ticklore started with a simple frustration: every event we cared about ended with a ticket in the trash. We thought that ticket deserved better.

Where the idea came from

Ticklore grew out of work with Virginia Beach Recovery Events (VBRE), an organization that runs alcohol-free gatherings, retreats, and community events for people in recovery. Every event mattered — sometimes more than any other in a person's year — but the ticket that got someone through the door was gone the next morning, same as any other.

At the same time, we kept running into the same gap from the fundraising side: donors want to know where their money goes, and small organizers rarely have a good way to show them. We started asking what it would take to fix both problems with one idea.

"If the night mattered, the ticket should too."

That's the whole premise. Every ticket Ticklore issues is a one-of-one digital keepsake — not a financial asset, not a speculative token, just a permanent, verifiable record that says: I was there, and here's what it meant.

What we believe

01

Keep it simple

No wallets, no crypto jargon. If an attendee has to think about blockchain, we've already gotten it wrong.

02

Keep it honest

Where money changes hands — especially for a cause — the ticket should show exactly where it went.

03

Keep it real

We started with one real partner and one real event, not a hundred imagined ones. Everything else follows from there.

Ownership

Where your ticket actually lives

A ticket that's supposed to last means nothing if you never really own it. Here's how we think about that, today and going forward.

DEFAULT

Held for you

Most people who buy a Ticklore ticket will never touch a wallet or hear the word "crypto." We hold it for you automatically — you get an email, a login, and a ticket that's yours to view anytime. This is what makes buying a ticket as easy as it should be.

OPTIONAL

Hold it yourself

If you already use a crypto wallet, or ever want to, you'll be able to move your ticket out of Ticklore and into your own wallet — permanently. Once it's there, it's genuinely yours. We don't hold it, control it, or need to stay in business for you to keep it.

"We think self-custody should be a choice you can make, not a requirement standing between you and a ticket."

Right now, personal crypto wallets are still unfamiliar to most people — so we're not going to force anyone to set one up just to attend a family reunion. But crypto is moving quickly, and a wallet may well be as ordinary as a bank app within a few years. We're building the option in from the start, so Ticklore can grow into that future instead of retrofitting for it later.

The vision

The story doesn't end when the event does

A ticket usually stops mattering the moment the event is over. We don't think it has to. Here's a piece of where we want to take Ticklore next.

BEFORE — LOCKED FOREVER

Chapter One

The moment your ticket is minted, its core facts — the event, the date, your seat or role — are written permanently. Nothing about that original record can ever be changed or erased.

AFTER — ADDED OVER TIME

The chapters that follow

Once the event happens, organizers can add to the story — photos, an attendee roster, highlights, a closing note. New entries only get added, never edited or removed, so the ticket keeps growing without ever rewriting what came before.

"A paper ticket ends the night it's used. We want a Ticklore ticket to keep writing itself."

This is a vision-stage idea, not something live in the first version of the product — but it's part of why we're building the way we are from the start. A family reunion ticket that shows the whole family's photos months later. A fundraiser ticket that shows exactly what the donation made possible. That's the direction we're headed.
